Research Question vs. Hypothesis

Research Question: An open-ended, clear, focused inquiry regarding a relationship between phenomena.
Example: "How does sleep duration affect exam performance?"

Hypothesis: A specific, testable prediction statement asserting expected direction and magnitude.
Example: "Students who sleep 8 hours will score 15% higher than those sleeping 5 hours."

Falsifiability Standard
Must be empirically testable

The Experimental Variables

Independent Variable (IV): The cause manipulated or categorized by the researcher.
Dependent Variable (DV): The measured effect or outcome.
Controlled Variables (CV): Extraneous factors kept strictly constant to ensure internal validity.

Experimental Control Ratio
High Internal Validity

Operational Definitions

Psychological constructs like "anxiety", "smart", or "frustration" are abstract. An operational definition explicitly details exact measurement steps, units, or instruments so independent researchers can replicate the study with identical accuracy.

Replication Precision
Exact Protocol Alignment

Modeling: Turning Broad Topics into Testable Designs

STAGE 1: BROAD

General Topic

"Social media and youth mental health."

❌ Unmeasurable / Vague
STAGE 2: FOCUSED

Research Question

"Does nighttime screen exposure affect adolescent sleep quality?"

⚠️ Better, needs units
STAGE 3: HYPOTHESIS

Testable Statement

"Adolescents using phones >2 hours before sleep will show 30% fewer REM sleep minutes."

✓ Falsifiable Prediction
STAGE 4: OPERATIONAL

Operational Variables

IV: Phone screen time (0h vs 2h).
DV: Minutes in REM sleep (EEG device).
CV: Room temp, caffeine.

✓ Fully Replicable

10-Min Guided Activity

Question Refinement Workshop

Transform vague, unmeasurable topics into focused, operationally defined research questions and testable hypotheses.

Broad Question #1

"Is social media bad for teenagers?"

Improve this question into a measurable research question. Specify the platform, usage metric, and psychological outcome.

Broad Question #2

"Does music make people study better?"

Improve this question by defining the type of music, the study task, and how performance will be measured.
